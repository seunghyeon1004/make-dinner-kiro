Rebuild is the incremental build of this build system: during cooking something slips, and the plan is recomputed with done and running steps pinned, moving only what has to move. Read docs/BRIEF.md ("Rebuild"), .kiro/steering/scheduler-guarantees.md (R1 to R6 and the notation), and the scheduler spec in .kiro/specs/scheduler/ (the Schedule type, timeline helpers, passes, oracles, generators and fixtures; reuse them, do not redefine them). Follow the format of the scheduler spec (EARS requirements with ids, design with real paths and exact signatures, algorithms with stated tie-breaks, a "Why the guarantees hold" section checked against the algorithm, hand-worked examples, correctness properties with ids and generators, test-first tasks.md; "(decided here)" marks; "Conflicts found"; decisions and open questions at the end of your reply).

Scope:
- `rebuild(schedule, progress, now)` in src/core/rebuild.ts, pure: forward repair, the new serving minute, re-align (right-justify of pending steps whose result does not keep, toward the new serving minute), lanes, readout, and the diff. Typed Result errors for bad input (unknown step id in progress, a done step with end before start, `now` not an integer, a running step that started after now, a pending step whose prerequisite is... decide what is and is not an error when the cook has done things out of order, and say why: the cook is always right about what already happened, so pinned facts are never rejected for breaking precedence or capacity; the rebuild plans the future around them).
- The exact Progress type and the Rebuilt type: the new Schedule (same shape as the scheduler's, on the same axis, with serving minute S' carried explicitly; say how every consumer that assumed "serving is minute 0" reads it), plus `diff: { moved: [{ stepId, fromStartMin, toStartMin }], unchanged: string[], servingShiftMin }`.
- Rebuild must be repeatable: the output of one rebuild is valid input to the next (the cook slips twice). State the invariant that makes this true and add a property for it (P-RB ids): a second rebuild with no further slip changes nothing (this is R4 applied to a rebuilt schedule).
- "Needs more time" on a running step (extraMin), a step finished early, a step started early by the cook, a step not started although its time has passed (the cook is behind: forward repair pushes it to now), a done step that took longer than planned, all steps done (nothing to plan; the serving minute is when the last terminal step ended).
- Lanes after a rebuild: pinned steps keep their lanes where possible; say what happens when actual times make two pinned steps overlap on one lane (reality over the model: capacity is only guaranteed from `now` onward, R1).
- Slack and waits in the rebuilt schedule are computed against S'. hold_exceeded warnings are recomputed (a held-back dish may now break a hold; that is exactly what the cook needs to be told).
- Hand-worked examples on the SUNDAY_ROAST fixture with HOME_KITCHEN, starting from table H1 of the scheduler design: (a) at now = -60 the roast is running and gets extraMin 15: show which steps move and that `greens.shred` (already done) and the pending prep stay; S' = 15; (b) the cook is 8 minutes behind on a hands-on step with nothing else slipping; (c) a no-slip rebuild (R4). Each as a full table the implementation must reproduce exactly.
- Properties: one per R1 to R6, using `arbProgress(schedule)` and `arbNow(schedule)` generators (define them: progress consistent with some moment `now` of a real run, including early starts, overruns and extra minutes), with oracles independent of src/. Extend test/meta/guarantees.test.ts by adding the `rebuild: ['R']` claim.
- Check R1 to R6 against the algorithm while writing the design, as the scheduler spec did, and report any conflict with a counterexample rather than adjusting quietly. In particular check R3 together with re-align, R5 together with repeated rebuilds, and whether forward repair in previous-start order can ever place a step before a prerequisite that was pushed later.

Keep tasks.md to about 20 sub-tasks.

Length: this spec builds on two others, so refer to them instead of repeating them. Aim for requirements.md under 160 lines and design.md under 420 lines; be exact rather than long.
